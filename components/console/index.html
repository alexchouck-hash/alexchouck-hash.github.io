About consoles and capital hardware The console is usually the part of a system that is designed last and lives longest, and its constraints come from the room rather than from the procedure.

The enclosure is a set of compromises

It has to be cleanable with the same aggressive chemistry as everything else in the room, which rules out many finishes and most exposed fasteners. It has to move, which sets castor size and where the mass can sit. And it has to dissipate heat without drawing dirty air across anything that matters.

These pull against each other: sealed is cleanable but hard to cool, and vented cools but collects.

Cables and connectors are the wear items

The enclosure will usually outlast every cable attached to it. Flex life at the strain relief, and how easily a damaged cable can be replaced in the field, are worth deciding early — they are cheap to design in and expensive to retrofit.

Anything patient-contacting or crossing an isolation boundary carries requirements the rest of the harness does not, so it is worth knowing which conductors those are before the harness is drawn.

Standards that actually apply

IEC 60601-1
Basic safety and essential performance of medical electrical equipment. The general standard, and the one that governs the isolation strategy.
IEC 60601-1-2
Electromagnetic disturbances — the collateral standard that usually drives cable, shield and connector choices.

Listed because they bear on the choices above — not as a compliance checklist, and not as advice about which apply to your device.
