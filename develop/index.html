Six weeks from kickoff to handover. The sprint documents design controls; it does not perform your verification testing. That is a separate line, and the two are designed to hand off cleanly.

What you receive

  • A Class II design history file in your templates: design plan, inputs, outputs, reviews, verification and validation plans, and design transfer records.
  • A live trace matrix linking inputs to outputs, verification, validation, and risk controls.
  • A risk management file structured to ISO 14971, populated with your hazards and controls.
  • A gap list naming every element that is blocked on test data or supplier information, so nothing is quietly marked complete.
  • The human-review record (below).

What the human-review record contains

  • The named reviewer and date for every document delivered.
  • Every AI-drafted section that was materially rewritten on review.
  • Source references for each design input we did not receive directly from you.
  • Decisions we escalated to you rather than resolving ourselves, and what you decided.

Questions we get

What do we need to have ready at kickoff?

Design inputs in whatever form they exist — a requirements spreadsheet, a marketing requirements document, or engineers who can answer questions for half a day. Your SOPs and document templates. An intended-use statement. Your risk file if you have one. If your inputs are not yet a document, drafting them is the first week of the sprint, not a blocker.

What does "submission-ready" promise, and what does it not?

It promises a file whose design-control documentation is complete, internally consistent, traceable, and formatted to your quality system, so it can be assembled into a submission without reconstruction. It does not promise clearance, and no one can. It also does not manufacture test data: sections that depend on verification results you have not generated yet are delivered with the data field left open and flagged on the gap list.

How does verification testing fit in?

The sprint produces the verification plan and the protocol structure; executing the testing is a separate engagement. If you want the two joined, the V&V line runs the readiness scan against the plan this sprint produces. See /verify.

Will the documents fit our SOPs and numbering?

Yes — that is the point of taking your templates at kickoff. Documents are generated into your formats with your numbering scheme and approval blocks, so they enter your eQMS as native artefacts rather than as PDFs somebody has to retype.

Who actually reviews the AI-drafted documents?

A named engineer or RA/QA reviewer, identified per document in the review record, who signs before the document reaches you. AI drafts and maintains consistency across the file; humans make the judgement calls and own the signature.

What if our device turns out not to be Class II?

We say so at kickoff, before pricing. Classification is part of the first conversation — a fixed price on the wrong pathway is worth nothing to either of us.
