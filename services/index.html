About Rapid Prototyping & Feasibility Builds (Engineering Primer & Selection Guide) Expand ↓

Prototyping in MedTech is not just 3D printing a shape — it is proving clinical physics before committing to tooling.

Feasibility vs. Verification Builds

Feasibility prototypes test whether a mechanism or shaft architecture functions in an anatomical model. They do not require validated tooling or formal DHF protocols, which is why they turn in days rather than months.

However, once a device enters formal Design Verification (DV), every prototype must be produced under validated processes, documented in the Device Master Record, and built in qualified cleanrooms.

Quick-Turn Sourcing Discipline

The biggest delay in catheter and instrument prototyping is custom polymer extrusion lead time. Choosing off-the-shelf stock sizes for the first iteration saves 3 to 6 weeks of die fabrication.

About 3D Printing & Additive Manufacturing (Engineering Primer & Selection Guide) Expand ↓

Metal 3D printing enables bone-integrating trabecular lattices impossible with CNC machining.

Porous Titanium vs Traditional Plasma Spray

Additive manufacturing allows osteointegrative pore sizes (300–600 µm with 60–80% porosity) to be integrated continuously throughout an implant, eliminating the risk of coating delamination associated with plasma spray.

Post-processing is critical: un-sintered powder trapped inside deep pores presents a severe particulate risk. Validated ultrasonic passivation and CT verification are mandatory under FDA guidance.

About Drafting, CAD & DHF Documentation (Engineering Primer & Selection Guide) Expand ↓

In medical manufacturing, an ambiguous drawing is the #1 cause of supplier rework and nonconformance.

GD&T Eliminates Coordinate Tolerancing Traps

Using plus/minus square tolerance zones on round medical features creates uncontrolled corner tolerances that either reject good parts or allow mating components to bind.

True position with Maximum Material Condition (MMC) datum references gives contract machine shops manufacturing leeway while guaranteeing assembly fit.

Medical machining differs from aerospace in one key respect: every edge touches living tissue or surgeon gloves.

Burr Control and Edge Breaks

A microscopic burr undetectable to the naked eye can peel a PTFE catheter liner or cause tissue tearing. Medical machine shops use specialized thermal deburring, abrasive flow machining (AFM), and electrochemical deburring (ECD).

Cut Patterns: Spiral vs. Interrupted vs. Interlocking

A continuous spiral cut maximizes flexibility but transmits low torque in reverse rotation.

An interrupted spiral with un-cut backbone beams balances 1:1 torque transmission with controlled bending radius.

Consumer electronics follow IPC Class 2 where minor solder voids and partial barrel fill are acceptable.

Medical devices require IPC Class 3: 75% minimum through-hole barrel fill, zero component overhang, and 100% 3D X-ray inspection of leadless ball grid arrays (BGAs) to prevent latent joint failure under transport vibration.

About Accelerated Aging & Real-Time Shelf Life Testing (Engineering Primer & Selection Guide) Expand ↓

You cannot stamp a 3-year expiration date on a sterile device without proving the sterile barrier holds for 3 years.

The Arrhenius Aging Equation ($Q_{10} = 2.0$)

Under ASTM F1980, the conservative aging factor ($Q_{10}$) is 2.0 — meaning every 10 °C increase in storage temperature doubles the chemical degradation rate.

Aging at 55 °C ($33 \text{ °C}$ above ambient $22 \text{ °C}$) yields an Acceleration Factor ($AAF$) of approximately $2^{3.3} \approx 9.85$, simulating 365 days of shelf life in just 37 days.

FDA requires parallel real-time ambient aging to run alongside accelerated aging until the full labeled shelf-life is reached.
