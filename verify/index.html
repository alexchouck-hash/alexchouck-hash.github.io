Questions we get

Which standards does the scan cover?

It covers the verification methods applicable to your requirements, which for most device programs means a mix of consensus standards and internal methods. Biocompatibility requirements are mapped against the ISO 10993 series, electrically active devices against the applicable IEC 60601 clauses, and mechanical requirements against ASTM or ISO methods where one exists. Where no consensus method applies, the scan says so and proposes an internal method with a written rationale rather than inventing a reference.

What exactly is in the deliverable?

A scored gap table covering every requirement in the list you send, a recommended test plan, a standards-reference sheet, and the human-review record. It is a document you can hand to your own test engineer and act on. It is not a protocol package — protocol drafting is the next engagement, priced separately.

Do we have to use your fixtures?

No. The scan tells you which methods are automation candidates and what a fixture would need to do; whether that fixture is ours, yours, or a contract lab’s is your call and does not change the price of the scan.

How do you justify sample sizes?

Sample-size rationale is drafted from the acceptance criterion, the requirement’s risk classification, and the statistical basis your quality system already accepts — attribute or variable, reliability and confidence targets, and whether the requirement is safety-related. Every rationale is reviewed and signed by a named engineer, and every one states its assumptions so your reviewer can disagree with the assumption rather than the number.

How is AI used, and how do you keep it out of the record?

AI drafts: it proposes method calls, acceptance criteria, and rationale text against the standards and your templates. It does not decide. A named engineer reviews every item before delivery, and the review record shows what was corrected. Your documents and data are not used to train any model, and are processed under the confidentiality terms in the SOW.

Can this extend to final / end-of-line inspection?

Yes, and it is the same closed loop one station further down the line. Machine-vision final inspection — including neural-network classifiers where the defect is easier to show than to specify — runs on hardware installed inside your building, so production images and models stay on your site and never leave it. That matters for IP and it removes a whole class of data-transfer questions from your quality agreement. The deliverable includes the test-method validation package: method description, the image and defect set used, acceptance criteria, repeatability and reproducibility results, and the human-review record, formatted so document control can file it. We describe what the station is built to do and how it was validated; we do not publish detection-rate claims, because those belong to your validated configuration and your product, not to a marketing page.

What if the scan finds our plan is fine?

Then the deliverable says so, and you have a defensible record that says so. We would rather hand you a short gap table than manufacture findings to justify the fee.
