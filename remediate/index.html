Questions we get

What does "scored" actually mean?

Each finding carries two numbers: audit exposure — how likely this is to be cited and how serious the citation would be — and remediation effort. The pair is what makes the report a plan instead of a list. The scoring rubric ships with the report, so your team can re-score a finding they read differently and see exactly which input they changed.

What happens to our file?

It is processed under NDA, held in an access-controlled workspace for the engagement, and deleted or returned on your instruction at the end. Your documents are not used to train any model, and are not shared with any other client. The specific retention and deletion terms are written into the SOW before you send anything.

Are the findings audit-defensible?

Every finding cites the clause it derives from, so it is defensible in the sense that matters: you can trace it to the regulation and argue it on the merits. What we cannot do — and no one honestly can — is guarantee how a particular investigator or notified body will read a particular file. The report is an input to your judgement, not a substitute for it.

How is AI used, and who checks it?

AI does the first read: it locates required elements across the file, flags missing or unapproved items, and drafts the finding text against the checklist. A named RA/QA reviewer verifies every finding before it enters the report, and the review record shows which AI findings were corrected or withdrawn. Nothing reaches you unverified.

What happens after the report?

Three options, and we are explicit that the first is fine by us: your team executes the plan; we execute it as a separate fixed-price engagement; or you use the report to scope the work with whoever you already trust. The scan is not priced as a loss-leader that only pays off if you hire us for the remediation.

Does this extend to post-market files, or only design history?

It extends, as an assessment scope. The same scored-gap discipline applies cleanly to complaint handling under 21 CFR 820.198: whether complaints are captured from every channel you actually receive them on, whether each record carries the investigation and the justification when no investigation was performed, whether the file links to CAPA and to risk, and whether the reportability decision is documented with its rationale rather than just its outcome. What you get is the same artefact — findings scored for audit exposure and effort, each citing its clause. What does not change is who decides: a reportability call is a named human’s decision on your side, made against your procedure, and nothing in this scope makes or automates that call. Ask us to scope it and we will tell you what a complaint-file assessment would cover for your volume.

Can you scan a file that is mid-remediation?

Yes, and it is a common case. Tell us what is already in flight and the report will separate "not started" from "in progress, not yet approved" rather than scoring your open work as a gap.
