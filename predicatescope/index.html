What it does

Predicate research is a genuinely tedious job: you find a cleared device, open its summary, find the predicate it names, open that one, and repeat until you have a picture of the lineage — or until you lose the thread. Most of a day, for something that is entirely public information.

PredicateScope walks that chain for you and draws it. For each device you get the clearances it descends from, the year each decision issued, and — this is the part that matters — a citation on every edge, so you can open the record and confirm the link yourself rather than trusting a graph.

A predicate graph you cannot verify is worse than no graph, because you will put it in a submission strategy. Every edge here resolves to a public record.

What it reads

The public openFDA 510(k) endpoint, which exposes the FDA's own clearance records — K-number, device name, applicant, product code, and decision dates. It is free, it needs no key for ordinary use, and it is the same data an FDA reviewer's public file rests on.

{{VERIFY_BEFORE_PUBLISH}} — Predicate relationships are named in the 510(k) summary documents, not exposed as a structured field in the API. How much of a lineage can be assembled automatically, and how much needs a human to read a summary, must be settled against the live API before this page makes any promise about coverage. Verify or cut the coverage sentences; do not soften this into a claim.

What it will not do

  • It will not tell you whether a predicate is appropriate for your device. That is a substantial-equivalence argument, it is judgement, and it belongs to your regulatory lead. The tool shows you what exists.
  • It will not see anything that is not public. If a relationship is not in the public record, it is not in the graph.
  • It will not take your data. You give it a device or a product code; there is nothing for us to keep.

Why we are giving it away

Because it is public data and a tedious job, and because the honest way to show you how we work is to hand you something that works rather than describe it. If it saves your team a day, that tells you more about whether to talk to us than any page on this site.

The same discipline that shows up in the tool — every claim cites its source — is the one that shows up in the DHF gap reports we charge for.
